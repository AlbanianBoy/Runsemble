# Runsemble — Full Concept Document
### "Because together is better."

---

## The Problem

Every year, millions of people set fitness goals they never start. Not because they lack discipline. Not because they don't care. But because they feel **alone**.

The gym is intimidating when you don't know anyone. Running outside at 7am feels pointless when there's nobody waiting for you. You scroll past a running app, see nothing but your own stats, and close it. The motivation dies quietly, not dramatically.

Meanwhile, three blocks away, someone else is sitting on their couch thinking the exact same thing.

Runsemble exists because that gap — between the person who wants to move and the person nearby who also wants to move — should not exist in 2025.

---

## What is Runsemble?

Runsemble is a **social fitness platform** built around one core belief: movement is better together.

It is not a tracking app. It is not a coaching app. It is not another leaderboard for people who are already fast.

It is the bridge between wanting to work out and actually doing it — because someone nearby is doing it too.

The name says it: an **ensemble** is a group that performs together. Nobody listens to a one-person orchestra. Nobody runs alone by choice.

---

## The Story

Picture Maya. She's 23, just moved to a new city for her master's degree. She used to run back home — it kept her sane, kept her social. Now she runs alone at 6pm, earns no Strava kudos, and quietly stopped going after week two.

Picture Jonas. Same city. He's been meaning to get back into shape since January. His problem isn't laziness — it's that going alone feels pointless. He needs a reason to show up. A person, not a plan.

Maya and Jonas live 400 meters apart. They run at the same time. They will never meet.

Runsemble fixes that.

Maya opens the app at 5:50pm. She sees a hotspot two streets over — a group run starts in 10 minutes. Six people are going. She hits "I'm joining." She gets a notification: "Jonas is also joining from nearby." They nod at the start line. They run. They add each other. Next Thursday, they're there again.

That's Runsemble.

## How a Person Opens the App — The Full User Journey

### Day 1: Onboarding

You download Runsemble. The first screen is not a form. It's a simple question:

> *"When did you last feel good after a workout?"*

You pick an option (yesterday / last week / it's been a while / I can't remember). The app responds warmly. It doesn't judge. It just says: *"Let's change that. Together."*

You set up a minimal profile:
- Name and photo
- Preferred sports (running to start, more later)
- Rough pace range (beginner / intermediate / any)
- When you usually like to move (morning / afternoon / evening)
- City/area

You get one permission prompt: location. The app explains exactly why — to show you runs and runners nearby — and gives you a privacy toggle immediately.

That's it. You're in.

### The Home Screen

The home screen has three zones:

**Top — "What's happening near you"**
A live strip showing:
- The next hotspot run starting near you (e.g. "Group run in Stadspark — starts in 18 min — 4 people joining")
- One nearby runner who's currently available (optional, privacy-controlled)
- Your current streak ("Day 3 🔥")

**Middle — The Feed**
A social feed of people in your groups and nearby community. Not stats dumps. Real moments:
- "First 5k without stopping 🥲"
- A photo from this morning's group run in the rain
- "Who's joining the hotspot at Berchem tonight?"

**Bottom nav — 5 tabs:**
Map / Feed / Hotspots / Groups / Profile

---

### The Map Tab

This is the heart of Runsemble.

You see a live map of your area with:

- **Hotspot markers** — orange pins pulsing gently, showing scheduled group runs. Each one shows: sport type, distance, start time, number of people joined, average pace.
- **Runner markers** — small avatar bubbles of people nearby who've toggled "I'm available." You can tap one and see their profile + send a run invite or a message.
- **Your status toggle** — bottom center. One tap: "I'm available to run." You set a time window (e.g. "for the next 45 min") and optionally a preferred distance. Done. You're on the map.

Privacy is built-in by default: locations are shown as approximate (rounded to the nearest 200m) unless you're in an active run session.

---

### The Hotspot Tab

Hotspots are the rhythm of Runsemble.

Every 30 minutes, across a city, a new scheduled run opens at a public location (park entrance, city square, trail start). These are auto-generated based on popular spots + user activity history.

On the Hotspot tab you see:
- A timeline of upcoming runs near you (next 3 hours)
- Each card shows: location, distance suggestion, pace range, who's already in
- One tap to join. You get a reminder 15 minutes before.

When you arrive, the app shows you who else is there through their avatar on the map. No awkward "who are you?" moments — everyone's profile is visible and you both know why you're there.

After the run, you can rate the experience (anonymously), and the app learns your preferences over time to suggest better matches.

---

### The Groups Tab

Sports groups are your permanent community inside Runsemble.

Groups can be:
- **Public** — anyone in the city can join (e.g. "Antwerp Morning Runners," "Beijing Expat Run Club")
- **Private** — invite-only (e.g. your uni friend group, your neighborhood crew)
- **University/organization-linked** — verified student groups

Inside a group:
- Shared calendar of runs and events
- Group chat
- Internal leaderboard (participation-based)
- Weekly recap ("Your group ran 47km together this week 💪")

Groups can challenge each other — e.g. "Ghent vs Antwerp: who logs more sessions this month?"

---

### The Profile & Rank System

Your profile shows:
- Total runs
- Total people run with (this matters — it's not just distance)
- Current rank and XP
- Badges earned
- Active streak

**The rank system** is Runsemble's most important design decision. Ranks are based on **participation**, not pace. This is intentional and non-negotiable. A 75-year-old who shows up every Tuesday earns the same XP as someone running 5-minute kilometres. The system rewards consistency, community, and effort — not athletic ability.

Rank tiers (example):
- 🌱 Starter
- 👟 Jogger
- ⚡ Pacer
- 🔥 Regular
- 🏅 Runsemble
- 👑 Elite Ensemble

XP is earned by:
- Joining hotspot runs (+50 XP)
- Running with someone new (+30 XP)
- Maintaining a streak (+20 XP/day)
- Inviting someone who completes their first run (+100 XP)
- Group challenges (+variable)

---

### Notifications — The Anti-Excuse Engine

Runsemble's notification system is the product's retention engine. It's smart, not spammy.

- **Proximity alert**: "Someone 300m from you just set their status to 'available for a run' — want to join?"
- **Hotspot countdown**: "A run starts in Stadspark in 15 minutes — 3 people are going"
- **Streak protection**: "You haven't moved today. Your 5-day streak ends at midnight."
- **Social nudge**: "Jonas just joined the Thursday evening run — you two ran together last week"
- **Daily inspiration**: One short message every morning, personalized to your time preference

All notifications are fully customizable. No one gets spammed into deleting the app.

# Thesis & Research Angles
### For Master's in Financial Engineering — University of Antwerp
### (Designed to roll into a PhD)

The strategic advantage you have is rare: you're building a platform that generates real behavioral data on social interaction, participation, and network formation. That's publishable gold. Below are five research angles, each rooted in financial engineering methodology, each viable as a master's thesis, and each with a natural PhD extension.

---

## Research 1: Matching Market Design and Algorithmic Efficiency in Social Fitness Platforms

**The Question:**
How should a two-sided matching algorithm be designed to maximize participation and retention in a location-based fitness app — and does compatibility-based matching outperform random or proximity-only matching?

**Why it fits Financial Engineering:**
Matching markets are a core topic in mechanism design and market microstructure — the same theory behind matching in financial markets (order books, clearinghouses, auction design) applies here. You'd apply Gale-Shapley matching, deferred acceptance algorithms, and multi-attribute preference models to a non-financial context.

**What you'd do on Runsemble:**
Design and A/B test two matching mechanisms — one purely proximity-based, one compatibility-weighted (pace, schedule, distance preference). Measure retention, run completion, and social tie formation across groups.

**Methodology:**
Randomized controlled experiment, structural estimation of preference parameters, simulation of algorithm variants.

**PhD Extension:**
Extend to dynamic matching under incomplete information — how does the platform learn preferences over time (bandit algorithms, reinforcement learning)? Publish in operations research or information systems journals.

---

## Research 2: Network Effects, Critical Mass, and the Cold-Start Problem in Two-Sided Platforms

**The Question:**
What is the minimum viable user density required for a location-based social fitness platform to sustain organic growth — and how do local network effects drive or suppress adoption across geographic micro-markets?

**Why it fits Financial Engineering:**
This is platform economics — a hot topic in digital markets, with direct parallels to liquidity theory in financial markets. A market with no liquidity (no users nearby) fails just like a thin financial market. You'd model adoption dynamics similarly to asset price diffusion.

**What you'd do on Runsemble:**
Track adoption across different city districts in Antwerp or Beijing. Measure whether activity in one neighborhood spills over to adjacent ones. Model the density threshold at which the platform becomes self-sustaining (the "tipping point").

**Methodology:**
Spatial econometrics, threshold models, hazard models for platform adoption. Potentially a Bass diffusion model adapted for geo-local network effects.

**PhD Extension:**
Multi-city rollout strategy optimization — how should a platform sequence city launches to maximize cross-market learning while minimizing cold-start losses? Connects to real options theory and sequential investment under uncertainty.

---

## Research 3: Behavioral Incentive Design — Does Gamification Increase Physical Activity, and What Mechanism Drives It?

**The Question:**
Do participation-based gamification systems (XP, streaks, ranks) increase exercise frequency and long-term retention — and is the mechanism social comparison, intrinsic motivation, or loss aversion?

**Why it fits Financial Engineering:**
This is behavioral economics applied to incentive design — the same toolkit used to analyze financial decision-making (prospect theory, loss aversion, present bias, commitment devices). Streaks are essentially commitment contracts. Ranks are relative performance benchmarks.

**What you'd do on Runsemble:**
Run a natural experiment: roll out the streak feature to one cohort, withhold it from a control group. Measure run frequency before and after. Separately measure whether losing a streak causes a spike in activity the next day (loss aversion signal).

**Methodology:**
Difference-in-differences (DiD), regression discontinuity at streak loss events, survival analysis for churn.

**PhD Extension:**
Optimal incentive contract design for health behavior — when do extrinsic rewards crowd out intrinsic motivation, and how do you design around that? High relevance for health economics and insurance markets (behavioral insurance products).

---

## Research 4: Social Capital Formation Through Repeated Physical Co-Presence — A Network Economics Approach

**The Question:**
Do repeated co-participation in physical activity (running together multiple times) create durable social ties — and does the strength of those ties predict long-term platform retention and physical activity levels?

**Why it fits Financial Engineering:**
This connects to social capital theory and network economics — how relationships form, strengthen, and generate value. In financial markets, relationship banking and trust networks follow similar dynamics. You'd model Runsemble's social graph and analyze tie formation and durability.

**What you'd do on Runsemble:**
Build the social interaction graph from the app's data (who ran with whom, how many times, over what period). Measure whether dyads who run together 3+ times have significantly higher 6-month retention than those who ran once. Test whether group membership mediates this effect.

**Methodology:**
Panel data models, survival analysis, social network analysis (degree centrality, tie strength), instrumental variable estimation (use proximity as an instrument for social interaction).

**PhD Extension:**
Does social capital formed through physical co-presence transfer to other domains (job referrals, trust in economic transactions)? Links to labor economics and social economics literature. High publishability in top economics journals.

---

## Research 5: The Economics of Spontaneity — Willingness to Participate and Real-Time Decision-Making in Social Fitness Markets

**The Question:**
What factors determine whether a person accepts a spontaneous run invitation on a fitness platform — and how do time pressure, social visibility, and reciprocity norms affect real-time participation decisions?

**Why it fits Financial Engineering:**
This is decision-making under uncertainty with time constraints — structurally similar to real-time trading decisions, option exercise problems, and dynamic programming under deadline pressure. You'd model the "accept/decline" decision as an optimal stopping problem.

**What you'd do on Runsemble:**
Log every instance where a user receives a proximity notification or run invitation. Record whether they accept or decline, and collect contextual variables (time of day, weather, how many others are going, prior relationship with the inviter). Build a predictive model of acceptance probability.

**Methodology:**
Discrete choice models (logit/probit), survival analysis for response time, structural estimation of a dynamic participation model.

**PhD Extension:**
Platform mechanism design — how should the platform time and frame notifications to maximize acceptance rates without triggering notification fatigue? This is a principal-agent problem with applications to digital market design broadly.

---

## Which Thesis to Pick?

If you want the cleanest path to a PhD in financial economics or quantitative economics, **Research 2 (Network Effects & Cold-Start)** or **Research 5 (Real-Time Decision-Making)** are most technically rigorous and closest to financial engineering's core toolkit.

If you want the most publishable and attention-grabbing combination of novelty + data, **Research 1 (Matching Design)** is your best bet — it sits at the intersection of computer science, economics, and behavioral data, which is exactly where top journals are hungry for empirical work right now.

If your supervisor is more behavioral/applied, **Research 3 (Gamification & Incentives)** is the most immediately tractable because you can design the experiment precisely and cleanly within your 3-month Beijing window.

All five can be written in a way that opens the door to a PhD — the key is framing your master's thesis not as a conclusion, but as "Study 1 of 3" in a larger research program.
